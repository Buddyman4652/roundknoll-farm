How the model is built

The timeline steps through the recorded subdivision plats, drawn over the 1957 landscape. From 1961 on, today’s houses appear in the year the county records say they were built (Maryland SDAT year built, matched by street address; a few outbuildings take their house’s year). The Section 1 and Section 2 plat lines share one colour.

The ground photo fades from the 1957 aerial to the 1964 and then the 1971 aerials (each covers the middle of the map; the edges keep the older photo). The terrain changes patch by patch: each lot switches from the 1957 surface to today’s lidar in the year before its house was built, and the roads are cut in the year after their plat was approved (1962, 1966, 1968, 1973), ahead of the houses. The 2023 aerial replaces the older photos on the regraded patches after 1972, starting in black and white to match the older prints.

Layers. “Colourise photos” gives the old photos an estimated colour borrowed from today’s aerial (interpretive, not original). “All plats over today” draws every plat at once over the current ground. “Original plat prints” drapes the recorded plat sheets (Sections 1 to 3 together) over the ground, warped to fit the map. “Raw lidar” is a hillshade of the Baltimore County lidar surface, trees and roofs included. “Topographic contours” are drawn from the same lidar every 5 ft (heavier every 25 ft), and show the 1957 ground’s contours before it was regraded.

Quality settings (top right, under Layers). “Larger textures” and “AI-enhanced aerials” load 4096 px copies of the four aerials; the AI version is upscaled with Real-ESRGAN and adds plausible detail that is not in the original photos. “Sharpen aerials” applies an unsharp mask. “Finer terrain mesh” uses an 800 × 800 ground grid built from the 1 m lidar. “Lidar-placed trees” puts a tree at each crown found in the lidar canopy model (about 6,800), coloured from the 2023 aerial; “Detailed tree shapes” swaps the simple blobs and cones for lobed crowns and layered conifers. The last three are heavier, so turn them off if the headset or your computer struggles.

Terrain is lidar from 2014 with later regrading removed for 1957. Trees and farm buildings in 1957 are drafted from the aerial photo and are approximate.

About the farm

This was one farm of about 153 acres on Sweet Air Road, Baldwin, in Baltimore County. The deeds trace it back to a survey of Davis Powell’s land in 1851. George W. Powell bought it in 1860, lost it to foreclosure in 1869, and Samuel Meakin, the high bidder at the auction, resold it to John Pocock that November. The Pocock family kept it for about fifty years. The 1860 census already shows John Pocock in the same district, with his wife Mary and eight children, among them Jesse and Eugene. In 1881 it was divided into two lots: Lot 1, about 92 acres, went to Eugene Pocock, and Lot 2, about 60 acres, stayed with John Pocock’s estate and passed to Salem Wesley Pocock in 1884.

The farmhouse, which the family dates to 1902, appears to stand on Lot 1, so it was built in Eugene Pocock’s time. Henry Albright bought Lot 1 in 1908 and Lot 2 in 1919, putting the farm back together, and his estate sold it to Charles and Dorothy Cook in 1946. A 2014 article on the house describes a working dairy farm, and the 1957 aerial photograph shows fields, tree lines and farm buildings around the house.

In 1959 the Cooks sold the whole farm to J. Thomas Requard & Co., Inc., which turned it into the Sweet Air Manor subdivision. Four plats were recorded between 1961 and 1972, the roads were cut in stages, and houses went up along them through the 1960s and 1970s. When the company was dissolved in 1968 Requard took the land that was left, and in 1975 he sold the old farmhouse lot (Lot 3, Block C, Section 2) to Ronald and Victoria Lessa. Jean D. Crockett owned the house from 1977 to 2014, then Nathan and Lauren Wright for a year, and the Kirkpatricks since 2015.

Which lot held the farmhouse is worked out from the bearings in the 1881 and 1884 deeds, fitted to the 1957 aerial, so it is probable rather than proven. The Owner history tab has the deeds one by one.
